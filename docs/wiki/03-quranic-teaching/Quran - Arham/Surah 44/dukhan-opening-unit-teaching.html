AD-DUKHĀN · THE SMOKE
The Opening Unit — Verses 1–6 · child-level teaching notes

One arc in six verses: the letters → the oath → the night → the writing → the source → the reason. Everything descends from Allah; everything descends as mercy.

✦ ◆ ✦
Note I · Verses 1–2

The Mysterious Letters & the Oath

❧❧ ❧❧
حمۤ ﴿١﴾ وَٱلۡكِتَـٰبِ ٱلۡمُبِینِ ﴿٢﴾
"Ḥā Mīm. By the Clear Book."
AD-DUKHĀN 44 : 1–2

The doorway of the surah. Two verses, two wonders: letters nobody can fully explain, and an oath sworn by the Book itself.

i. What is "Ḥā Mīm"?

Just two letters of the alphabet — like saying "H. M." Twenty-nine surahs open with letters like these, and the honest answer of the scholars is: Allah knows best what they mean. But Ibn ʿĀshūr points to what they do: they are a challenge. The Qurʾān is built from the very same letters the Arabs used every day — ح، م، ا، ل — yet nobody could ever build anything like it.

🗣 For your child

"Everyone has the same 26 letters, but nobody writes like Shakespeare. Everyone had the same Arabic letters — ḥā, mīm — but nobody could make anything like the Qurʾān. It is as if Allah opens the surah saying: here are the letters, they are on your tongue every day — now try. Nobody ever could."

◈ Nuance

It is also good to teach humility here — the ṣaḥāba and great scholars did not claim to know the meaning of these letters with certainty. Saying "Allah knows best" about some things is part of knowledge, not a failure of it.

ii. Why does Allah swear an oath — and by the Book?

The wāw in وَٱلۡكِتَـٰبِ is the wāw of oath — "by the Clear Book." People swear by what is great and dear. When Allah swears by the Qurʾān, He is announcing its greatness before saying anything else. And here is the marvel Rāzī noticed: Allah swears by the Book about the Book — like a person of honor saying "I ask you, by your own honor." The Qurʾān is so noble it can vouch for itself.

🗣 For your child

"When people want you to really believe them, they swear by something precious: 'by my mother!' Allah opens this surah with an oath, and the precious thing He swears by is the Qurʾān itself. That tells you, before anything else is said: this Book is the treasure."

iii. What does "Mubīn" — Clear — mean?

Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives two readings that are both true: the Book is clear in itself (its truth shines), and it makes things clear — it shows people the path, the ḥalāl and ḥarām, where they came from and where they are going. A lamp is bright in itself, and it lights the room.

🗣 For your child

"A flashlight does two things at once: it is bright, and it shows you the way in the dark. The Qurʾān is Mubīn like that — bright in itself, and lighting the road for us."

◈ Nuance

Rāzī records that "the Book" could also mean the earlier revealed books or the Preserved Tablet — but the dominant reading (Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān: الظاهر) is the Qurʾān itself, since the very next verse says "We sent it down."

☾ One-minute bedtime version

"This surah opens with a riddle and a promise. The riddle: two little letters, Ḥā Mīm — the same letters everyone uses, yet nobody could ever write a book like this one. The promise: Allah swears an oath — and He swears by the Qurʾān itself, the way you swear by the most precious thing you have. Then He calls it the Clear Book: bright like a lamp, and lighting our way like one too."

SOURCE BASIS — Ibn ʿĀshūr (taḥaddī function of the muqaṭṭaʿāt), Rāzī (oath-by-itself analogy; kitāb referent options), Ibn ʿAṭiyya (mubīn dual reading), Zamakhsharī (wāw qasam), Abū Ḥayyān (al-ẓāhir: the Qurʾān).

Note II · Verse 3

The Blessed Night

❧❧ ❧❧
إِنَّاۤ أَنزَلۡنَـٰهُ فِی لَیۡلَةࣲ مُّبَـٰرَكَةٍۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا مُنذِرِینَ ﴿٣﴾
"We sent it down in a blessed night — surely, We have always been warners."
AD-DUKHĀN 44 : 3

Three ideas a child can hold: the gift (Qurʾān), the night (Laylat al-Qadr), the reason (Allah's mercy expressed as warning).

i. Who is "We" and what is "it"?

"We" is Allah speaking. When Allah says "We" instead of "I," it is like a king speaking — it shows greatness, not that there is more than one. "It" is the Qurʾān — the verse right before mentions "the Clear Book," so "it" points back to that.

🗣 For your child

"Allah is telling us about the most precious gift He ever sent to people — the Qurʾān — and He is telling us when He sent it, because the timing itself is special."

ii. What is the "blessed night"?

The heart of the verse. Almost all the great scholars — Ṭabarī, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr — say this night is Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power in Ramaḍān. How do they know? The Qurʾān explains itself. Elsewhere Allah says the exact same words: "Indeed We sent it down in Laylat al-Qadr" (97:1). And in al-Baqarah He says the Qurʾān was sent down in "the month of Ramaḍān" (2:185). Put the three verses together like puzzle pieces.

🗣 For your child

"It is like Allah gave us three clues in three different places, and when you put the clues together, you find the treasure: the night the Qurʾān came down is the Night of Power in Ramaḍān — the night that is better than a thousand months."

◈ Nuance

A few early scholars (like ʿIkrima) thought it was mid-Shaʿbān — Laylat al-Barāʾa. The strongest scholars checked the evidence and said no: the Qurʾān itself says Ramaḍān, and the Shaʿbān reports are not strong enough to argue with the Qurʾān's own words. Ibn Kathīr: whoever says Shaʿbān has "wandered far from the well." Mid-Shaʿbān may still be honored — but this verse is about Laylat al-Qadr.

iii. One night — yet twenty-three years?

The question a smart 10-year-old will actually ask — and the same question a man once asked Ibn ʿAbbās. His answer became the classic explanation: the Qurʾān came down in two stages. First, the whole Qurʾān descended in one night — from the Preserved Tablet down to the lowest heaven. Then, from there, Jibrīl brought it to the Prophet ﷺ piece by piece over 23 years.

🗣 For your child

"Imagine a whole library being delivered to a house near your school in one single night. Then, every day for years, the librarian brings you exactly the book you need for that day. The library arrived all at once — but you received it lesson by lesson, because that is how you learn."

◈ Nuance

Ibn ʿĀshūr read it slightly differently — "We sent it down that night" means the first revelation began that night, in the cave of Ḥirāʾ, and every verse after joined it like drops joining a river. Both readings agree on the night; they differ only on the mechanism. Either way you teach it, you are on solid ground.

iv. Why "blessed" (mubāraka)?

A beautiful subtlety hides in the Arabic grammar. The word مُبَارَكَة is a passive form — "a night that was made blessed." The night is not magical by itself. Allah chose it and poured blessing into it — and the greatest blessing He poured into it was the Qurʾān itself. Rāzī made this point with pure logic: time is just time; if a night is noble, it must be because of what happened in it — and nothing greater ever happened in any night than the Qurʾān coming down.

🗣 For your child

"Why is your birthday special? The day itself is 24 hours like any other day. It is special because of what happened on it — you arrived! This night is the most blessed night ever because the best thing that ever came to humanity arrived in it."

v. "We have always been warners"

Notice Allah does not say "We warned once." The Arabic كُنَّا مُنذِرِينَ means "warning has always been Our way." This answers the hidden question why send the Qurʾān at all? Because Allah, out of mercy, never leaves people walking toward danger without telling them. A warner is not someone angry at you — a warner is someone who loves you enough to shout before you get hurt.

🗣 For your child

"If you were about to cross a road and a truck was coming, and I saw it — would I stay quiet? Never. I would shout your name. That is what a warner is. Allah saw people heading toward harm, and because He is kind, He has always sent warnings — through every prophet, and finally through the Qurʾān. A warning is actually a gift of love."

✦ The verse in one breath

The Qurʾān — this Book We swear by — We sent it down in the greatest night of the year, a night We filled with blessing, and We did it because We never stop caring enough to warn.

☾ One-minute bedtime version

"A very long time ago, in the quietest, most special night of Ramaḍān, Allah sent His greatest gift down from the highest heaven — the Qurʾān. He picked that night and filled it with blessing, the way we fill a room with light for someone we love. And why did He send it? Because Allah never lets the people He loves walk into danger without calling out to them. The Qurʾān is Allah calling out to us — every single day."

SOURCE BASIS — Ṭabarī, Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ālūsī — full eight-scholar synthesis from qr_tafsir_entries.

Note III · Verse 4

The Night the Year Is Written

❧❧ ❧❧
فِیهَا یُفۡرَقُ كُلُّ أَمۡرٍ حَكِیمٍ ﴿٤﴾
"In it every wise matter is made distinct."
AD-DUKHĀN 44 : 4

Verse 3 told us which night; verse 4 tells us what happens in it besides the Qurʾān coming down. This is the verse about how Allah runs the coming year.

i. What does "yufraq" mean?

The word یُفۡرَقُ comes from the same root as "furqān" — to separate, to spell out clearly. The scholars (Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) explain: on this night, everything decreed for the coming year is separated out from Allah's great record and written down in detail — item by item, name by name.

🗣 For your child

"Imagine the biggest school in the world. The head teacher has one master book with everything in it. Then, one special night each year, the plans for the coming year are copied out — class by class, student by student — and handed to the teachers. Laylat al-Qadr is the night Allah's plans for the whole year are written out and handed to the angels."

ii. What exactly gets written?

Ibn ʿAbbās lists it: who will be born this year, who will pass away, what provision each person will receive, even the rain — and amazingly, even the pilgrims: "so-and-so will do Ḥajj, and so-and-so will do Ḥajj," written by name. Ḥasan al-Baṣrī was asked: does this happen every Ramaḍān? He answered with an oath: "Yes, by Allah — it is in every Ramaḍān."

🗣 For your child

"On that one night, this year's list is written: the babies who will come, the rain that will fall, what each family will earn — even the names of the people who will stand at Ḥajj this year. Your name might be on lists you know nothing about!"

iii. Why "ḥakīm" — a wise matter?

Ḥakīm here means muḥkam — firm, sealed, precise. Ālūsī gives the sharpest explanation: while a decree is still with Allah in the Preserved Tablet, He "erases what He wills and confirms" (13:39). But once it is written out on this night and handed to the angels, it does not change — it is sealed for delivery. And "wise" also because every single item carries wisdom: nothing on the list is random.

🗣 For your child

"When the plan leaves Allah's book and goes to the angels, it is like a sealed envelope — nobody can change what is inside. And everything inside was chosen with perfect wisdom, the way a doctor measures medicine exactly — nothing extra, nothing missing, nothing by accident."

◈ Nuance — for the parent

Mujāhid was asked about the duʿāʾ "O Allah, if my name is among the wretched, erase it and write me among the felicitous." He pointed to this verse — the yearly operations (provision, hardship) are re-issued each year; but the book of ultimate felicity and wretchedness is fixed — ثابت لا يُغيَّر. Two registers: the annual working ledger, and the eternal decree. Do not collapse them when teaching.

iv. The angels receive their assignments

Some scholars (transmitted by Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Ālūsī as a report, not a certainty) describe the delivery: provisions to Mīkāʾīl; wars, earthquakes and lightning to Jibrīl; deeds to Ismāʿīl, the great angel of the lowest heaven; calamities to the Angel of Death. Teach it as "the scholars mention" — a beautiful picture of an organized kingdom, not a verse of the Qurʾān itself.

🗣 For your child

"Allah's kingdom is more organized than any army and any company. Each great angel receives his own sealed list for the year: one carries the provisions, one the weather and the great events, one records the deeds. Nothing in the universe just happens — everything is assigned, carried, and delivered."

v. Why this makes it the night of duʿāʾ

Connect the dots for the child: if the coming year is written out on this night, and Allah hears every request before the ink is set — then this is the night to ask. This is why the Prophet ﷺ sought it in the last ten nights. Ibn ʿĀshūr: the verb is present-tense (yufraq) — it keeps happening, every year, on the anniversary of the Qurʾān's descent.

🗣 For your child

"If you knew the exact night the whole year gets planned, and you knew the King listens especially closely that night — would you sleep through it? That is why we search for Laylat al-Qadr and ask Allah for everything good: the year is being written, and duʿāʾ reaches the Writer."

✦ The two verses together

Verse 3: the greatest gift came down in the greatest night. Verse 4: and in that same night, every year, the coming year is written with perfect wisdom. The night of revelation is also the night of destiny.

☾ One-minute bedtime version

"Remember the special night the Qurʾān came down? Something else happens on that night — every single year. Allah's plans for the whole coming year are written out: the babies, the rain, what people will have, even who will visit His House. Each great angel receives a sealed list to carry. Nothing is random, nothing is forgotten — and on that night, Allah listens extra closely to everyone who asks. So when that night comes, we stay awake and we ask."

SOURCE BASIS — Ibn ʿAbbās (year-list incl. Ḥajj roster), Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (every Ramaḍān), Mujāhid (two-registers guardrail), Ālūsī (muḥkam-after-release), angelic registers as transmitted report. Eight-scholar synthesis.

Note IV · Verses 5–6

A Command from Us, a Mercy from Your Lord

❧❧ ❧❧
أَمۡرࣰا مِّنۡ عِندِنَاۤۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا مُرۡسِلِینَ ﴿٥﴾ رَحۡمَةࣰ مِّن رَّبِّكَۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِیعُ ٱلۡعَلِیمُ ﴿٦﴾
"A command from Us — surely We have always been senders — a mercy from your Lord. Indeed He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing."
AD-DUKHĀN 44 : 5–6

The close of the opening unit. Verse 4 said the year's matters are written; verses 5–6 tell us where they come from (directly from Allah) and why it all happens (mercy).

i. "A command from Us" — why repeat "amr"?

Verse 4 already said "every wise matter (amr)." Now Allah says it again: "an amr from Us." Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the repetition: it is for majesty (tafkhīm). First the decree was called wise; now it is stamped with its origin — from Our presence, مِّنۡ عِندِنَا. Not from angels, not from stars, not from luck — from the King Himself.

🗣 For your child

"When a letter arrives from the palace, the envelope says: FROM THE OFFICE OF THE KING. That is what 'from Us' means. Everything written on that night carries Allah's own seal — it comes straight from Him, not from anyone in between."

ii. "We have always been senders"

The twin of "We have always been warners" in verse 3. Sending messengers is Allah's constant way — Ādam, Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, and finally Muḥammad ﷺ. Ibn Kathīr adds: the need was pressing — people had lost the way, so the Messenger came reciting clear verses. Allah never left any people without guidance reaching them.

🗣 For your child

"In the whole story of the world, Allah never went silent. Every time people forgot the way, He sent someone to remind them — prophet after prophet, like lighthouse after lighthouse along a dark coast, so no ship is ever without a light."

iii. "A mercy from your Lord" — the reason behind everything

Grammar carries the meaning here. "Raḥmatan" answers why: We sent it all — the Book, the messengers, the decrees — for mercy (the scholars call this mafʿūl lahu, the "because-of" word). And notice: Allah does not say "a mercy from Us" but "a mercy from your Lord." Zamakhsharī and Ibn ʿĀshūr both stop at this: being a Rabb — the One who raises, feeds, and cares — by its very nature brings mercy. A true caretaker cannot help but be merciful.

🗣 For your child

"Why does a mother pack your lunch, check your homework, and call out when you are near the road? One reason sits under all of them: love. This verse tells us the one reason under everything Allah does for us — the Book, the prophets, the plans for every year: MERCY. And He says 'your Lord' — the One who has been taking care of you since before you could talk."

◈ Nuance

"Min rabbika" — your Lord — turns to address the Prophet ﷺ personally (Ibn ʿĀshūr: the address shifts to honor him, since the sending is mercy and he himself is the mercy: "We sent you only as a mercy to the worlds," 21:107). Teaching hook: the Messenger is not just the carrier of the mercy — he is part of the mercy.

iv. "The All-Hearing, the All-Knowing" — mercy that fits perfectly

Rāzī closes the circle beautifully: people in need either say their need — and He is al-Samīʿ, He hears every word — or they cannot even put it into words — and He is al-ʿAlīm, He knows it anyway. So His mercy always fits the need exactly, spoken or unspoken. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds: "He and only He" (the emphatic huwa) — not the idols people were calling on, which hear nothing and know nothing.

🗣 For your child

"Sometimes you tell your mother what you need. Sometimes you do not even have the words — and she still knows. Allah is like that, but perfectly: He hears every duʿāʾ you say out loud, and He knows the ones stuck in your heart that you cannot say. Nobody else — nothing else — can do that."

✦ The opening unit, complete

Six verses, one arc: the letters (a challenge) → the oath (by the Book itself) → the night (Laylat al-Qadr, the gift) → the writing (the year decreed with wisdom) → the source (from Us directly) → the reason (mercy, from a Lord who hears and knows). Everything descends from Allah; everything descends as mercy.

☾ One-minute bedtime version

"Remember the night the year gets written? Tonight's verses answer two questions. Where do those plans come from? Straight from Allah — sealed from the King Himself, nobody in between. And why does He do all of it — the Book, the prophets, the plans? One word: mercy. He is your Lord — the One who has cared for you since before you were born. He hears every wish you say out loud, and He knows the ones you cannot even find words for. So you are never, ever unheard."

SOURCE BASIS — Ibn ʿĀshūr (iʿādat "amran" li-tafkhīm; iẓhār "rabbika"; Samīʿ/ʿAlīm as taʿlīl + ḥaṣr), Zamakhsharī (raḥmatan mafʿūl lahu; rubūbiyya entails raḥma), Rāzī (spoken/unspoken needs), Ibn Kathīr (the pressing need), Ṭabarī (naṣb readings). Completes the 44:1–6 series.
